# Shipaton 2025: what the judges actually rewarded

*Sources: the [2025 rules](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2025.devpost.com/rules), all [30 winning Devpost pages](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2025.devpost.com/project-gallery), and RevenueCat's own [winners post](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/company/shipaton-2025-winners), which explains why each winner won. Next Gen didn't exist in 2025, so this shows what the same company's judges value, not the Next Gen scoring itself.*

## What judges focus on most

Ranked by how often it shows up in RevenueCat's write-ups of the 30 winners:

1. **The founder's story: who built it and why.** Nearly every winner's write-up opens with it:
   - Heartbeat Hero: the founder's firefighter uncle saved a life with CPR.
   - Hearing Buddy: built for the developer's partner, who is hard of hearing.
   - MoodHaven: the developer's 7-year-old son.
   - Gurwi: the founder grew up in one of Colombia's poorest regions.
   - Vector Guard: a PhD student who studies these diseases.
   - PitchLab: built by a professional pitcher.

   Judges remember people, then apps.
2. **Proof that real people use it.** Numbers appear even outside the growth categories:
   - Payout: 17,000 users, $30,017 revenue, 1,750 subscribers.
   - Gurwi: 13,000 users and 1,000+ reviews averaging 4.9.
   - ReadHim: $1,100 MRR in 10 days.
   - Shutter Declutter: 1,000+ paying subscribers.
   - Voicetree: 1,000 installs and $2,000 revenue.
   - Steps Share: users with a friend walked **22% more**, a result rather than a download count.
3. **One standout capability, explained concretely.**
   - Heartbeat Hero: ARKit plus IMU fusion measures compression depth "with millimeter accuracy".
   - PitchLab: a custom neural network plus a physics engine.
   - Dayloop: Vision face alignment.
   - Hearing Buddy: iOS 26 on-device speech plus CoreML speaker detection.

   Judges name the hard thing you solved.
4. **Monetization that fits the mission.**
   - **HAMM 1st prize went to Vector Guard's "1:50 Justice Model"**: every $2.99 subscription funds 50 free accounts in high-risk areas.
   - Heartbeat Hero gives students full access free.
   - SkillMe (Design, 2nd) personalizes its paywall copy to the user's goal ("Don't lose your progress on becoming the next Gordon Ramsay").
   - Napkinmatic mixes credits with subscriptions.
5. **Product thinking and learning from users.**
   - Voicetree: "defining a clear MVP, prioritizing user trust, validating traction".
   - Echo: tested in coffee shops and fixed onboarding from what they saw.
   - Friendy+: pivoted after feedback.
   - Cooked This: tuned reminders "to avoid fatigue".
6. **Privacy and on-device processing, plus Apple's newest frameworks.** Dayloop ("photos remain local"), Heartbeat Hero (fully offline), Hearing Buddy and Posturely all cite this.
7. **Public write-ups count.** Tomo Japan's Medium posts, Voicetree's blog post and Friendy+'s six-part YouTube series are mentioned by name.

### What doesn't matter

| Thing people assume matters | 2025 evidence |
|---|---|
| Devpost likes | Winners had **max 8, median 2** likes |
| Big team | **21 of 30** winners were solo |
| Hand-written code | 12 of 30 mention AI coding tools. The **$65k Grand Prize** went to Payout, where "not a single line of code was written by hand" |
| Tech stack | Native Swift 13, React Native/Expo 8, Kotlin Multiplatform 5 (mostly its own category), Flutter 4 |
| Very long write-ups | Median about 5,200 characters. The Grand Prize write-up was about 2,400 |

## Rules: 2025 vs 2026

| | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Submissions | 812 | **3,922** (about 4.8×) |
| Grand Prize | $65k "Build & Grow" (fastest growth after an early release) | $100k (most user traction and growth momentum) |
| Video | Judges watch up to **3 min**; 8 platforms allowed | **Under 2 min**, YouTube or Vimeo only (staff on Discord: 2:30 is OK) |
| Eligibility | Age of majority only | Adds **minors aged 13+, Next Gen only** |
| Store release | Required | Required, **except Next Gen** |
| Code repo | Not required | **Required for Next Gen** (public, open-source license) |
| Judging stages | Stage 1 pass/fail viability, then criteria, then rating | Stage wording removed |
| Categories | Build & Grow, #BuildInPublic, Buzziest Launch, HAMM, Design, Peace, Best Vibes, OneSignal, Kotlin Multiplatform, Staff | Adds **Next Gen**, Best Game, Catvertising, influencer awards, Samsung/Replit/Stripe/Layers/Noise. Drops Buzziest Launch and Best Vibes |
| Winners | 30 (3.7% of entries) | Next Gen: 3 places among about 409 eligible entries |

## What this means for the 2026 Next Gen prediction

The 2025 pattern **reinforces my top 3** in `winner_prediction.md`:

- **Konvo:** it matches the strongest winning pattern: a personal story, "my first mobile app", and hard numbers (2.9M views, 14.2K downloads), like Payout, ReadHim and Gurwi.
- **Guru:** it's close to a composite of three 2025 first-place winners. It has Gurwi's education-access story, Vector Guard's "paying users fund free users" model (that one won HAMM), and Heartbeat Hero's offline, free-for-students approach.
- **Larder:** it matches the Dayloop and Hearing Buddy pattern: on-device privacy, Apple's newest frameworks (Foundation Models, AlarmKit) and polish. Its student-priced semester plan echoes Heartbeat Hero.
- **Sailors:** it's strong on what wins design awards (mascot, haptics, widgets, a distinctive look, like Posturely or SkillMe). It's weak on the two things judges retell most: a founder story and proof that people use it.

**Caveat:** Next Gen is judged only on the video and repo, against its own four criteria, and possibly by different judges. These are patterns, not guarantees.

**Tally:** 9 strong, 4 medium, 13 light, **4 with no story** (Payout, Friendy+, DrawIt, Napkinmatic AI3D).

### What it shows

1. **Story isn't mandatory, but the 4 winners without one each had a hard, objective strength the category measures:**
   - Payout's revenue and users (the Grand Prize is judged on traction)
   - Napkinmatic's three-way monetization mix (HAMM)
   - DrawIt's three-platform execution (Kotlin Multiplatform)
   - Friendy+'s OneSignal Journeys plus a 6-part video series (OneSignal)
2. **No story-less entry won a subjective category.** Peace Prize, #BuildInPublic, Design and Best Vibes winners *all* had a personal why, and all 3 Peace winners had a strong one.
3. **Three of the four story-less winners placed below 1st** (Napkinmatic 2nd, Friendy+ 3rd, DrawIt 4th). The exception, Payout, had $30k in revenue to stand in for a story.
4. **Every winner gave judges *something* memorable to retell.** Where there was no origin story, RevenueCat's blurb led with a striking fact instead: "not a single line of code was written by hand" (Payout), "a six-part YouTube series… pivots" (Friendy+), "even supporting Apple Pencil" (DrawIt).

### What it means for 2026 Next Gen

Next Gen is judged on subjective criteria (idea, progress, RevenueCat use, craft), much like the 2025 categories where every winner had a story. An entry without one needs a hard, objective edge instead, such as Konvo's 10k downloads or Turn's measured evaluation.

**Sailors has neither:** no personal why, and no objective proof beyond completeness. Its monetization isn't explained and there's no usage data.

Re-weighting my rubric so story counts half as much changes nothing that matters: Larder stays 1st, the top 3 hold, and Sailors stays 15th. **What holds Sailors back most is the missing Next Gen case (it never says students or Next Gen), unexplained monetization, and how hard it is to verify**, more than the missing story alone.
