## What judges look for, in RevenueCat's own words

Charlie Chapman and Perttu Lähteenlahti (RevenueCat developer advocates who run the judging) explained the process in the [livestream](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HIyLyX8tkHs):

1. **Intake filter:** automatic checks. Next Gen needs "an open-source public repo… you've submitted the code before the end of September 30th" (29:16).
2. **Pre-screening:** RevenueCat staff watch **only the first 2 minutes** of the video, read the description, and grade each entry **1–5** per category. The top entries move on (31:56–38:56).
3. **Judge scoring:** judges watch the same 2 minutes and read the description. They're "not required to download the app". *"In the video specifically show how your app works. That's going to be the number one thing"* (39:21–40:12).
4. **Final review:** developer advocates "download every app, test them, see that they match what is in the video". Anything that doesn't match is disqualified (41:05).

From that, these are the questions a judge will be asking, with the weight I gave each:

| # | The judge's question | Where it comes from | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | **In the first 2 minutes, do I understand what it does, who it's for, and what makes it special?** | "This is what my app does, this is who it's for and this is what makes it special" (53:51) | ×2 |
| Q2 | **Is there a compelling story?** Why this builder, why this problem | "The apps that stand out are the ones that can tell a good compelling narrative" (52:37). Recording yourself is "probably more effective" (43:02) | ×2 |
| Q3 | **Does the video show the app actually working,** so I don't need to install it? | "That's going to be the number one thing the judges… will be using" (40:06) | ×2 |
| Q4 | **Does it make the case for this category?** Who built it, how, and that it's entering Next Gen | "Mention the categories you're targeting, what your app does, and how you've built it" (33:10) | ×1 |
| Q5 | **Is the monetization novel or tailored to the app's audience, and is it explained?** | "The baseline… it's not just a subscription with an annual or monthly plan… something that's kind of novel or more specific to your app or audience" (58:16) | ×2 |
| Q6 | **Does it zoom in on one or two specific details** that show care? | "Zoom in on details… here's an example of a small detail that I really focused on" (55:35) | ×1 |
| Q7 | **Is it complete and polished?** Meaningful progress toward a working app | Next Gen criterion 2. Rhys on Discord: "lean more towards trying to complete the app" | ×2 |
| Q8 | **Is the write-up authentic and concise?** | "A very long description can actually work against you" (34:10). "Fully AI-written text is usually quite tedious" (36:06) | ×1 |
| Q9 | **Will it survive final verification?** App matches the video, code in by Sep 30, judges can run it | Final review (41:05), Next Gen code deadline (29:16) | ×1 |

Scores are 0–5 per question, weighted and scaled to 100.

## What the category asks for

From the [official rules](https://revenuecat-shipaton-2026.devpost.com/rules):

- **Who:** active students (high school, university, bootcamp or another academic program) with a qualifying student email. Ages 13 and up; minors need parent or guardian consent.
- **What to submit:** a demo video under 2 minutes (judges don't have to watch past 2:00) and a **public, open-source repo with a license that shows in the repo's About section**. No store release or paid developer account is needed.
- **Judging criteria:**
  1. Is the idea clear, useful, interesting or original? Does it solve a real problem?
  2. Does it show meaningful progress toward a working app, with the core functionality clear from the video and repo?
  3. Does it use RevenueCat thoughtfully (subscriptions, IAP, web purchases, ads…)?
  4. Does it show thoughtful technical choices, product thinking, and care in how it's built and presented?
- **Prizes:** 1st gets $20,000, a Shippy trophy, a Times Square billboard, a 9to5Mac/9to5Google spotlight and an App Growth Annual invite. 2nd gets $10,000 and 3rd gets $5,000.

## How I judged

1. **Scanned the whole gallery:** all 164 pages and 3,922 projects. **699** link to GitHub, the one thing only Next Gen requires. Full list: `nextgen_submissions.md` and `site/index.html`.
2. **Ran hard checks on all 699** using the GitHub API, YouTube and Vimeo:

| Check | Result |
|---|---|
| Repo exists and is public | 662 ✅ · 37 missing, private or profile-only ❌ |
| Open-source license GitHub can detect | 399 ✅ (MIT 339, Apache 26, AGPL 20, GPL 12, other 2) · 19 unrecognised ⚠️ · 9 **proprietary** ❌ · 235 none ❌ |
| RevenueCat SDK actually in the code | 516 ✅ |
| Video works | 678 ✅ · 9 links aren't videos (a channel handle was pasted) · 12 unavailable on YouTube |
| Video under 2:00 | 213 run longer than 2:00 ⚠️ |
| No commits after the Sep 30 deadline | 236 committed after it ⚠️ (rules: "you may not make any changes" after the Submission Period) |
| **Passes every hard check** | **409** |

3. **Pre-screened** the 409 with a heuristic for each criterion: RevenueCat in code, code size and commit history, tests, how complete the write-up is, video length, and stated student status.
4. **Read the top ~60 write-ups by hand** and ranked them on the four criteria. The heuristic only picked what to read. Turn, for example, ranked #116 on it and is #4 here.

**Caveat:** student status can't be verified from public data. I relied on what entrants say about themselves.

### 🥇 Larder, Joshua Samuel (high school student)
[Devpost](https://devpost.com/software/larder-snap-your-fridge-cook-what-you-ve-got) · [GitHub (MIT)](https://github.com/TheQuantum-Dev/Larder) · [Video, 1:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=walqKykWk9g)

## Why strong entries will lose points

- **No open-source license, or a proprietary one.** This is the most common failure: 235 repos have none and 9 use custom "all rights reserved" licenses. Some of the most-liked Next Gen entries fall here: **Aýkitap** (❤18, no license), **MeteoWatch** (❤13, no license), **LangCity** (❤9, no license, and the repo is a release repo rather than source). The exception is **Sailors** (❤23). Its view-only license was approved by RevenueCat staff on the Shipaton Discord before the deadline, so it stays in contention, now at #12 on the judges' rubric. Other entrants with custom licenses may have similar approvals I can't see.
- **Videos over 2 minutes.** 213 entries, including Bubble Games (2:53). RevenueCat staff said on Discord that a 2:30 video is fine, so this isn't a disqualifier. But the rules still say judges don't have to watch past 2:00, so anything important after that may go unseen.
- **Broken videos.** 9 links point to a YouTube channel instead of a video, and 12 videos are unavailable.
- **Commits after the deadline.** 236 entries. Most judges probably won't disqualify for it, but it's an easy tiebreaker.
- **Not actually Next Gen.** Some entrants link GitHub but say they're in other categories, for example Lauk, Lunker and Nanti (entered in Influencer, Peace and HAMM) and The Blind Spot (Peace Prize). They're kept in the list per the GitHub rule but left out of this prediction.

## What RevenueCat staff said on Discord

From Shipaton Discord threads shared with me by an entrant. I can't open Discord myself to verify them.

- **Execution counts slightly more than the idea.** Rhys, Sep 22, answering a question about Next Gen: polish, UX, core functionality, money-making potential, uniqueness and attention to detail "will be taken into account". On idea vs execution: "a balance… I'd lean more towards trying to complete the app."
- **Judges try the app hands-on.** Jaewoong, Sep 27: in a later stage judges use the app to check it "work[s] as shown in your submission". "Bugs, lag, or responsiveness can matter if they noticeably affect the overall experience."
- **The paywall doesn't have to be in the video, Test Store purchases are fine for Next Gen, and a 2:30 video is acceptable.** Rhys, Oct 1.
- **A view-but-don't-sell license is fine.** Rhys, Sep 28.
